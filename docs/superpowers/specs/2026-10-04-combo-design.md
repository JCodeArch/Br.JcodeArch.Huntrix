# Combo, Hit Stop, and Impact Feedback — Card 25

## Goal

Extend the base attack from card #24 with configurable ground and air combo chains, a global hit-stop response, and a typed impact notification that presentation code can consume.

## Accepted decisions

- A `CombatComboDefinition` owns two explicit sequences: grounded and aerial. Each sequence contains at least two authored `ComboStep` entries; each entry references one valid `AttackDefinition` and defines its chain input window and hit-stop duration. No example asset, attack values, timing, or final balance is authored by this card.
- The caller supplies the current grounded state when a chain starts. `AttackController2D.TryStartAttack(float facingDirection)` remains compatible and starts the grounded sequence; a new overload accepts `bool isGrounded`. Grounded state is read only while idle; while a chain is active that argument is ignored and the captured sequence stays fixed. A fresh idle chain selects again. Facing direction is validated and recaptured from each accepted chain press for that next step.
- Each new logical Attack press inside the current step's configured chain window immediately cancels that step and starts the next. Windows use the step elapsed value accumulated by scaled `FixedUpdate` and sampled at the most recently completed fixed tick. The interval is semi-open `[start, end)`: start is included and end is excluded; fixed-step sampling quantizes when a configured boundary becomes observable. Presses outside the window are discarded; there is no input queue or hold-repeat. A whiff does not prevent a valid chain input. Each press advances at most one step. During hit stop the elapsed value freezes; a press inside the still-open window is accepted and advances state while physics remains stopped, while a press outside is discarded and never queued.
- Chain windows are authored in seconds relative to the step start and must be finite, non-empty, and within the finite total startup + active + recovery duration. The final step has no next link; its window fields are ignored even if malformed and do not invalidate the definition.
- Each step starts a fresh hitbox activation and therefore permits at most one accepted hit per receiver for that step. A target may be hit again by a later step. Existing self/faction, health, dash invulnerability, persistent-overlap, and knockback rules remain unchanged.
- Hit stop is a short global game-time freeze requested only after a receiver accepts damage. A single `HitStopService` is bootstrapped before gameplay scenes and is the designated project owner of `Time.timeScale`; it offers `SetPaused(bool)` so future pause UI uses the same owner. Unexpected direct writes while a stop is active are detected as ownership loss when the value becomes nonzero. It measures the hit-stop deadline with unscaled time and extends overlapping requests to `max(existing deadline, request deadline)`. While paused, hit stop overlays remain pending but do not unpause; after resume, an unexpired stop still freezes simulation. A new hit-stop request made while paused is ignored; an existing hit-stop deadline stays pending across a pause. If an external direct write changes `Time.timeScale` to a nonzero value while the service expects zero, including during pause or hit stop, the service relinquishes all time-scale ownership, preserves that external value, clears its paused/frozen ownership state, and discards any pending hit-stop deadline so it cannot unexpectedly freeze time later. If disabled or destroyed while it still owns a pause or freeze, it deterministically releases ownership by restoring the captured base scale and clearing pause/deadline state, even if it was paused; it does not change `Time.fixedDeltaTime`.
- `AttackHitbox2D` is the sole publisher of an internal accepted-hit notification after receiver acceptance and deduplication. `AttackController2D` is the sole public publisher of typed `CombatImpactEvent` instances: it subscribes to its own hitbox exactly once while enabled, unsubscribes while disabled, and forwards the payload. Presentation listeners subscribe to the controller event and follow the same enable/disable lifecycle. The event carries attacker, receiver, attack definition, and zero-based combo step index. It omits a contact point because a trigger overlap has no unique physical contact. Rejected, duplicate, same-faction, self, dead, or dash-invulnerable contacts emit no public event. The receiver is added to the step deduplication set before the internal accepted-hit notification is emitted; controller handling then requests hit stop before synchronous public event dispatch. Listener exceptions are isolated per listener: a throwing listener cannot escape into hit processing, duplicate damage on re-entry, or suppress hit stop or subsequent listeners. VFX, SFX, animation, authored assets, and scene wiring remain for later content/integration cards.
- Card #26 continues to own parry, counterattack, and rewards. Final combo balance, device performance targets, network/co-op policy, and physical input bindings remain open.

## Runtime contracts

- `CombatComboDefinition.IsValid(out string error)` validates both sequences contain at least two steps, every referenced attack, finite nonnegative hit-stop durations, finite attack total duration, and each non-final chain window against its attack duration. Final-step window fields are deliberately ignored.
- `AttackController2D` advances configured phases and a step elapsed clock on `FixedUpdate`; global hit stop pauses those scaled-time phases and the clock along with physics. `TryStartAttack(float facingDirection)` starts grounded; the overload with `isGrounded` samples state only while idle. A valid chain press uses the captured context, recaptures facing for the next step, and ends the current hitbox before beginning it. Link checks sample the latest completed fixed-step elapsed value with `[start,end)` semantics.
- `AttackHitbox2D` continues to own accepted-target deduplication and emits only an internal accepted-hit notification after `DamageReceiver2D.TryReceiveHit` returns true. The enabled `AttackController2D` handles that notification in one order: request hit stop, then publish its public typed impact event. The controller subscribes once while enabled; on disable it cancels the active attack and disables the hitbox before unsubscribing. Thus a disabled controller cannot leave an active hitbox processing hits; a disabled hitbox processes no contacts and emits no accepted-hit notification.
- `HitStopService` bootstraps one persistent runner before scene gameplay (with an editor/test fallback), prevents duplicates, and uses one unscaled deadline rather than per-hit coroutines. It owns a base scale and pause flag; only its API writes `Time.timeScale`. It restores the latest base scale after stop/resume. A nonzero direct external write while the service expects zero, whether paused or frozen, is treated as loss of all ownership: preserve that value, clear pause/freeze state, and discard pending deadline. Disabling/destroying the runner while it still owns pause/freeze deterministically restores its captured base scale and clears pause/deadline state, even when paused; it clears its static instance, and test teardown restores global time state.

## Verification

EditMode validates combo definitions, invalid references/windows/durations, and final-step handling. PlayMode covers both sequence selections, full chains, one-step-per-press behavior, start-inclusive/end-exclusive link boundaries using fixed-step elapsed samples, discarded early/late inputs, whiff chaining, fixed context through landing/takeoff, recaptured facing, input accepted only when the link is open during hit stop, hitbox teardown on cancel/disable/despawn, per-step hit dedupe, and all card #24 combat regressions. Hit-stop tests verify accepted-hit-only requests, max-deadline overlap, prior non-default scale restoration, and pending hit-stop across pause/resume in both deadline cases: resuming before the unscaled deadline reapplies the remaining freeze; resuming after the deadline restores base scale without starting a freeze. They also verify pre-paused no-op, nonzero external scale change relinquishing ownership during hit stop and during pause (including clearing any pending deadline), unchanged fixed delta, disable/destroy cleanup while paused and while frozen, no duplicate runner, and test restoration. Impact-event tests verify the hitbox-to-controller single notification/subscription boundary, controller-to-listener public publishing lifecycle and order (hit stop before event), payload, exactly-once dispatch after dedupe, absence for rejected contacts, no accepted-hit processing while controller/hitbox is disabled, listener disable/re-enable/re-entry behavior, and isolation of a throwing listener while confirming accepted damage, per-step dedupe, and hit-stop still occur exactly as specified. Domain reload recovery is covered by a PlayMode test that simulates static subsystem reset and verifies a surviving runner rebinds without retaining stale ownership or deadline. The final full EditMode/PlayMode suites and exact result counts are recorded in the plan and Trello card before integration.

## Risks and limits

Global hit stop freezes all local game-time simulation, including every character and enemy; this is a provisional readability choice for the current single-player prototype, not an approved policy for local co-op. Revisit global hit stop before implementing local co-op because freezing all players together may not suit that mode. Any future pause, slow-motion, or network simulation must coordinate through the same time-scale owner. Without approved Android/iOS targets and budgets, static review and Unity tests do not prove device performance. Impact dispatch makes no explicit per-impact allocation while collections and listener snapshots are stable; receiver-set capacity can grow for new targets, and the listener snapshot is rebuilt when subscriptions change. Impact notification is a contract only; this card adds no licensed or authored presentation content.
